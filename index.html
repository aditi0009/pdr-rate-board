    How the board works

    Every report is one reaction in one or more databases or networks, with what was found and, where possible, the rate as given, a suggested rate and a reference. The board is live: what one group adds, every open page sees within seconds.

    Issue types

    TypeMeaning
    missingThe reaction is not in the network although it matters in PDR conditions.
    wrongThe rate coefficient, its temperature dependence or its branching disagrees with the measurement or calculation it cites, or with other databases, by more than the stated uncertainty.
    recheckSuspicious but not settled: an odd temperature extrapolation, a rate copied between databases without a source, an abundance effect that nobody has traced to a reaction yet.
    updateA newer measurement or calculation exists and the database still carries the old value.

    Statuses

    openReported, nobody has checked it yet.
    under reviewA group is checking it. Say so in a comment.
    confirmedA second group has reproduced it.
    resolvedFixed in the database, or shown to be fine, with the reason in the comments.
    disputedGroups disagree. Keep the argument in the comments until it settles.

    Rate notation

    Rates are written in the modified Arrhenius form used by UDfA and KIDA, k = α (T/300 K)β exp(−γ/T), with α in cm³ s⁻¹ for two-body reactions and s⁻¹ for photo- and cosmic-ray processes. For photoreactions give α as the unshielded rate in the Draine field and note the attenuation exponent in the description. If a database uses a different form, say which.

    Who can write

    Signed-in members of the hosting organisation and invited contributors can add reports, confirm them, comment and change statuses. Visitors arriving through a public link can read everything but not write. Each report records who added it; confirmations are one per person.

    Good practice

    One reaction per report. Quote the database entry number or line where you can. Give the condition under which the effect was seen, density, temperature, field, since many rates only matter in one corner of PDR parameter space. When you resolve a report, say what changed and where.
